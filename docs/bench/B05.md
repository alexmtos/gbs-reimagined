# Bench log — B-05: the output program per source

Same bench as [M3](M3.md): RetroScaler GBS-C on COM9, joined to the home Wi-Fi (2.4 GHz, channel 6),
VGA→MS9288A→HDMI into an NZXT Signal HD60. Firmware 1.1.1 unless a section says otherwise.

**Question** ([roadmap](../roadmap.md), B-05): which output programs give a clean picture on this
board for each source, to set the default output (Auto) per standard.

**Method.** `tools/bench/program_matrix.py` sets each output resolution over the board's API with
frame lock on and integer scale off, waits for the lock and 20 s more for the HDMI chain, reads the
status (the input signature, the lock) and captures 30 frames of a still menu. The criterion is the
one in [videopath lessons §3.4](../research/gbs-control-lessons-videopath.md):

- **Frame lock** follows the source. When it is locked the status reports the source's rate as the
  output's, so the rate error in the result files is 0 by construction; the lock is the evidence.
- **Vertical ratio**, output lines per source line, within 0.5 % of a whole number. It comes from
  the program's vertical scale (console `status`), not from the captures: the capture card always
  delivers 1920x1080 and rescales the other modes itself.
- **No shimmer or tearing.** *Unstable share*: the share of lit pixels whose luma moves by more than
  8 (of 255) over the 30 frames. 1080p reads higher than the other modes on every source because it
  is the only one the card does not rescale, and rescaling softens edges.

Run folders are under `bench-runs/` (not versioned): `20261117-*-b05-<source>-<mode>`, with a
`20261117-*-b05-<source>.json` table per source.

## 2026-11-17 — 60 Hz sources

Sources: a PS1 on RGB (composite sync) with the 240p Test Suite menu, at 240p (261 lines) and at
480i; a Wii at 480p on component (525 lines). Every program locked, on every source.

| Output | Ratio, 240p | 240p | Ratio, 480i / 480p | 480i | Wii 480p |
|---|---|---:|---|---:|---:|
| 1280x960 | 4 | 0.0020 | 2 | 0.0071 | 0.0038 |
| 1280x1024 (4:3, 960 lines) | 4 | 0.0023 | 2 | 0.0064 | 0.0041 |
| 1080p | 4.5 | 0.0063 | 2.25 | 0.0109 | 0.0150 |
| 720p | 3 | 0.0046 | 1.5 | 0.0068 | 0.0056 |
| 480p | 2 | 0.0014 | 1 | 0.0061 | 0.0010 |

- The whole-frame mean moved by 0.02–0.15 levels over each run: no flicker or dropped frame.
- The unstable pixels sit on text edges, the blinking cursor and the gradient backgrounds, in every
  mode (each run's frames looked at): no tearing band, no shimmer across flat areas.
- **Every program locks and is stable.** By the ratio criterion, 1280x960, 1280x1024 (4:3) and 480p
  are whole multiples of both 240p and 480 lines, 720p of 240p only, and 1080p of neither: the
  reference project saw 1080p from 480 lines shimmer for that reason alone (§3.4). Here 1080p has
  the highest unstable share with every source, which agrees, but it is also the only mode the card
  does not rescale, so this bench cannot tell the two causes apart. **Integer scale** gives 1080p a
  whole multiple (4× for 240p).
- **Verdict:** 1280x960 is a whole multiple of both 240p (4×) and 480 lines (2×) and fills a 4:3
  frame: **Auto stays 1280x960 for 60 Hz sources.**

## 2026-11-17 — 50 Hz source

Source: a PS2 (PAL) on RGB (composite sync), 576i (311 lines per field, deinterlaced), on the Open
PS2 Loader menu. Every program locked.

| Output | Ratio, 576 lines | Unstable |
|---|---|---:|
| 1280x960 | 1.667 | 0.0107 (captured over USB, see the Wi-Fi section) |
| 1280x1024 (4:3, 960 lines) | 1.667 | 0.0074 |
| 1080p | 1.875 | 0.0359 |
| 720p | 1.25 | 0.0204 |
| 576p | 1 | 0.0045 |

- The unstable pixels are the horizontal edges of the menu text, the deinterlacer's usual flicker on
  a 576i source, and the selected entry; the whole-frame mean moved by 0.02–0.08 levels.
- **Every program locks and is stable**, but only 576p is a whole multiple of 576 lines (and of
  288: the others give 3.33, 3.75 and 2.5), so by the ratio criterion only 576p is clean with a
  50 Hz source. For 288p games, **Integer scale** gives 1080p (3×) and 720p (2×) a whole multiple.
- **Verdict:** 1280x1024 and 720p were the outputs that showed the whole picture as loaded on 1.1.1
  (next section); 1280x1024 gives a PC monitor its usual size and is not affected by the Wi-Fi
  problem below. **Auto stays 1280x1024 for 50 Hz sources** (the project owner's decision,
  2026-11-17).

### The top of the PAL picture was cut

**Symptom** (seen by the project owner on the HDMI output): at 1080p the top of the picture seemed
cut.

**Method.** The menu's dark grey background (level ~25) marks the edges of the PS2's picture, and
the menu text is the ruler: the top and bottom margins are taken between the background's edge and
the first or last row of text, as a share of the text's height. At 1280x1024, where the picture has
black above and below, they are 0.0317 and 0.0366: the whole picture. It is 959.5 output lines tall
there (1012 captured rows × 1024 / 1080), 575 source lines at the scale of 1.667: all 576 lines of
a PAL picture.

**On 1.1.1** the bottom margin was whole in every mode, the top was not, and the black left at the
bottom matched what was missing at the top: the picture sat too high.

| Output | Black above / below (rows of 1080 captured) | Top margin |
|---|---|---:|
| 1080p | 0 / 15 | 0.0168, about 15 rows cut |
| 1280x960 | 0 / 17 | 0.0149, about 17 rows cut |
| 1280x1024 | 21 / 47 | 0.0317, whole, off centre |
| 720p | 2 / 2 | 0.0277 |
| 576p | 0 / 69 | 0.0062, about 25 rows cut |

**No line is lost in the capture**: at 1080p, vertical position +4 shows the whole picture (top
margin 0.0307, bottom 0.0366, 1 / 0 black rows), +8 leaves black above and cuts the bottom, −4
cuts more of the top. The PAL programs' display windows start above the lines the HDMI converter
shows. The NTSC programs' windows are centred on them: the window's centre against the active
centre of the standard timing the converter recognises (CEA-861 / VESA DMT, from the vertical sync)
is 582.5 against 582 at 1080p, 519 against 520 at 1280x960, 553 against 554 at 1280x1024 and 388
against 386 at 720p.

**Steps that centre a full-height picture**, stepping the vertical position on each output
(`bench-runs/20261117-125*-palv-*`): 1080p +4 (1 / 0), 1280x960 +4 (0 / 1), 1280x1024 +3 (33 / 35),
720p 0 (2 / 2), 576p +12 (22 / 25).

**Fix**: the geometry starts each PAL program's picture that many source lines lower, before the
user's vertical position (`firmware/src/video/geometry.cpp`, `palCentreFor`). On the development
build (`bench-runs/20261117-1307*-palv-*-0`), with vertical position 0:

| Output | Black above / below | Top margin |
|---|---|---:|
| 1080p | 1 / 2 | 0.0287 |
| 1280x960 | 0 / 2 | 0.0287 |
| 1280x1024 | 31 / 36 | 0.0317 |
| 720p | 2 / 3 | 0.0268 |
| 576p | 22 / 27 | 0.0258 |

Outside 1280x1024 the top margin reads 0.003–0.006 below the reference even where black shows
above the picture (720p, 576p), so a difference of that size is the measurement itself: the
deinterlacer's flicker moves the text's first row by a row or two, and the card's rescaling softens
it. 1280x960 shows no black row above, so a captured row or two of its top may still be missing.

The [M2 notes](M2.md) of 2026-10-14 said all 576 lines showed: they measured the bright content
(level 40) and so missed the dark background.

**Force 60 Hz**, same source, unchanged by the fix (the 60 Hz programs are centred already): 1080p
and 1280x1024 whole, 1280x960 and 720p within 3 captured rows. **480p shows 480 of the 576 lines**:
the menu's title and bottom bar are cut. The 60 Hz 480p program is 1:1 and the scaler only enlarges
vertically (`VDS_VSCALE` 1024 is 1:1), so it cannot fit 576 lines in 480. The user guide says so
under Force 60 Hz, in
[Picture shape, position and size](../user-guide/en/README.md#picture-shape-position-and-size).

### Wi-Fi drops at 1280x960 with frame lock

**Symptom.** During the 50 Hz run the board stopped answering on the network right after the
first mode change, 1280x960 with frame lock on; the picture stayed.

**Not the firmware.** Over the serial console the main loop answered in 6 ms (median; at most
14 ms) in every mode, and the board stayed joined (−46 to −52 dBm). The radio loses the packets:
ping from the computer, with the router's ping as the control (20 / 20 every time):

| Output (PS2 PAL, frame lock on) | Board's ping |
|---|---|
| 1280x960 | 1/30, 17/30, 2/20, 0/20, 8/20 |
| 1080p | 30/30, 30/30, 20/20, 20/20, 20/20 |
| 1280x1024, 720p, 576p | 20/20 each, twice for 1280x1024 |
| 1280x960 with frame lock off | 20/20, 20/20 |

With frame lock off the TV5725 makes its own 108.000 MHz; with it on, the Si5351 drives the clock
pin at 108.451 MHz (VCO 867.6 MHz, MultiSynth ÷8). 1080p locks at 108.482 MHz (VCO 867.9 MHz) and
does not disturb the Wi-Fi. A spectrum measurement was not available.

**Mitigation tried: the Si5351's output drive and VCO.** A build for the experiment only (not kept)
cycled four settings every 45 s within one boot, at 1280x960 with frame lock on, the clock unchanged
(108.451 MHz), and the computer pinged 25 times in each period, 10 s after the change:

| Drive, VCO | Board's ping per period | Answered |
|---|---|---:|
| 6 mA, 867.6 MHz (÷8, as shipped) | 22, 17, 23, 16 of 25 | 78 % |
| 2 mA, 867.6 MHz | 22, 24, 24 | 93 % |
| 6 mA, 650.7 MHz (÷6) | 24, 22, 10 | 75 % |
| 2 mA, 650.7 MHz | 16, 20 | 72 % |

Standalone builds with 4 mA and 2 mA (one boot each) gave 4, 15, 15 and 20, 12, 13 of 20 (the 2 mA
boot joined another access point of the same network, at −69 dBm). The shipped setting itself
answered 78 % in that boot and 10–70 % in the boot after it (`2, 10, 14 of 20`, with 1080p at
20 / 20 in between). **No setting removed the loss beyond the spread between periods**, and any
change to the clock would also have to be verified at 161.6 MHz, where 1280x960 runs with a 60 Hz
source ([M1](M1.md)). The clock is unchanged; the user guide names the case and the way around it:
Auto (1280x1024), or frame lock off, with a 50 Hz console.

**Frequency sweep.** Another build for the experiment only, after the frame lock, set the Si5351
to one frequency of a list every 35 s; the computer pinged 20 times in each period, 6 s after the
change, and read the console's status after it (`pal-960p`, the Si5351 driving the scaler, in
every period). The control on 1.1.2 just before: 1280x960 locked at 108.451 MHz answered 10, 13
and 15 of 20, 1080p locked at 108.482 MHz 20 and 20.

| Si5351 (MHz) | First sweep | Second sweep, about 40 min later |
|---|---|---|
| 108.000 | 20 | |
| 108.300 | 11, 9 | |
| 108.400 | 1 | |
| 108.420 | 11 | |
| 108.430 | 19 | |
| 108.451 (where 1280x960 locks) | 12, 7 | 20, 20, 19 |
| 108.460 | 10, 14 | |
| 108.470 | 17 | |
| 108.482 (where 1080p locks) | 20, 20 | 20, 20, 20 |
| 108.500 | 7 | |
| 108.551, 108.597, 108.601, 108.605, 108.651, 108.702 | | 20, 20, 20 each |
| 108.600 | 20, 20 | |
| 109.000 | 8 | |
| 110.000 | 20 | |

The first sweep stopped on a serial-port error after 18 periods (107.5, 108.44 and 108.8 MHz were
not reached). The board stayed on channel 6 (−40 to −47 dBm in the readings around both). A
sweep made earlier with frame lock off by mistake (the setting had not been saved before the flash,
so the Si5351 did not drive the scaler) answered 20 / 20 everywhere and is left out.

**Diagnosis.**

- The drops are interference in the board's own 2.4 GHz receiver while the Si5351 drives the
  scaler: the main loop stayed at 6 ms, and with the TV5725's own 108.000 MHz (frame lock off) the
  Wi-Fi answered every time.
- They depend on the exact clock frequency: within one boot, frequencies a few kHz apart behaved
  differently (108.420 MHz 11, 108.430 MHz 19, 108.460 MHz 10 and 14), and the pattern is not one
  harmonic of the clock falling in channel 6 (2426–2448 MHz): 109.000 MHz, whose 22.5th harmonic
  is at 2452.5 MHz, lost packets too. 108.482 MHz, where 1080p locks with this console, answered
  20 / 20 (or 30 / 30) in all 21 periods measured that day.
- They also vary with time: the second sweep found no loss at any frequency, 108.451 MHz included,
  on the same channel at the same signal.
- Most likely, but not measured: narrowband spurs of the Si5351's fractional synthesis (the PLL
  runs at 34 + 0.704 times the 25 MHz crystal at 108.451 MHz), or their mixing with the scaler's
  clocks, falling in the receiver's band. A spur's place follows the exact fraction and would move
  with the crystal's drift, which would explain both the fine frequency dependence and the change
  over time. Confirming it needs a spectrum analyser near 2.44 GHz.
- **No firmware change** (the project owner's decision, 2026-11-17): the limitation stays in the
  user guide, with Auto (1280x1024) or frame lock off as the way around it.
